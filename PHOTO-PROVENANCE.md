# Photograph provenance

Both photographs are stock images from Unsplash, licensed free for commercial
use with no attribution required. Both are credited on the page anyway, and both
are labelled in their own caption as **not** photographs of this business.

### `assets/img/cookout.jpg` — 1700 × 1294
- Used on: `index.html` (full-bleed, first image)
- Source: Unsplash photo `8Cerf3zW8hA`
- Subject: barbecued chicken on a plate being handed across, cooked outdoors
- Chosen because it matches Brianna Kelly's review: "them good old cookout days"

### `assets/img/plate.jpg` — 1600 × 1066
- Used on: `food.html`
- Source: Unsplash photo `EuET7ZThg0c`
- Subject: a home-style plate of fried chicken with sweet potato fries

## Rejected during the build

- A Pexels plate (`6763224`) turned out to be a studio-lit Caesar salad on a
  black plate — wrong food, wrong register. Not used.
- An Unsplash cast-iron pan shot (`It0bkN5ClD8`) was a good dark-wood texture but
  the meat in the pan reads raw. Not appetising. Not used.
- Pexels' and Unsplash's search pages are both blocked from this environment;
  only the image CDNs and the `/download` redirect work, so photos can only be
  reached by ID found via web search.

## What to replace these with

Real photographs beat both, in this order of value:

1. **The plate as it is actually served** — the turkey wings and the meatloaf
   customers name. This is the single most valuable photo the business could take.
2. **The counter or window where you order**, so a first-timer knows what they
   are looking for inside a service station.
3. **The exterior from Bruton Blvd**, so people can recognise it while driving.
4. **The women who cook**, if they are willing — customers credit them directly
   and they are the most-praised thing after the food.

Anything 1600px or wider on the long edge is enough. Straight off a phone is fine.
The moment real photos exist, delete the "not a photograph of Sam's kitchen"
captions along with the stock files.
